A Trust Architecture for Pronto at Scale

The home is the most private environment a platform can enter. For the Pronto professional who enters it, trust means safety. For the customer who opens up their home, trust means informed consent: knowing whether they’re being recorded, if yes, what is recorded, what it becomes, and who controls it.

Designing with transparency for both is what it means to build trust at scale.

2
Safety Commitment Moment + Household Context Declaration
First booking initiation + checkout · Net new to category
Displayed exactly when the customer intends to make the first booking
"Help us make this a safe and positive experience for everyone."
Pronto professionals are verified, trained, and trusted. We ask our customers to meet them with the same respect.
This is not a T&Cs wall. It is a single full-screen moment, warm, human, brief, that signals to the customer that the professional's safety is a value Pronto holds actively.

It creates psychological accountability at the exact moment the professional's safety becomes real.

It frames what immediately follows, the two household context questions, as care rather than interrogation/ friction.
Question 1 — Booking intent · Single select · Mandatory
"Who are you booking for?"
🏠 Myself — I will be present 📍 Someone else — I am booking remotely
If "Someone else" is selected: receiver name, receiver contact number, and full service address are captured as a linked accountability record. Currently these may exist as three disconnected fields in the app with no linkage to the booker's verified identity.
Question 2 — Household context · Single select · Mandatory
"Who will be home during the appointment?"
👩 Only I will be present 👩 Female resident only 👨 Male resident only 🧓 Elderly female resident 🧓 Elderly male resident 👫 Mixed household — female and male members 👨‍👨 Multiple members — all male
The "Only I will be present" option is shown only when Q1 = Myself. "Multiple members — all male" surfaces the one household composition that is most safety-relevant to a lone woman professional, without the platform making a blocking decision on that basis.
What this does
    Removes the burden from the professional to independently assess the household situation before entering. The platform now carries that information systematically.

    For remote bookings, creates a named accountability record linking receiver identity, contact number, and service address as a single declared unit.
What this does not do
It does not verify the declaration. A bad actor can lie. It is limited as a declared intent record and a data instrument, not a prevention mechanism.

A false declaration does not prevent an incident, but it creates a documented accountability footprint that did not previously exist.
3
Professional Pre-Arrival Briefing + Door Confirmation
Pronto Professional app · At dispatch and at arrival · Net new to category
Before the professional begins travel, her app shows a structured briefing: the receiver name and contact, whether this is a self-booking or a remote booking, and the full household context declaration.

Critically, the briefing includes a soft pre-acceptance step. The professional sees the household context before the booking is confirmed to her. She can accept or flag for reassignment. No explanation required, no formal complaint process, no penalty. The platform does not make the safety call. The professional does.

At arrival, before the countdown timer starts, the professional confirms presence at the door. The timer is a product-enforced lock. It cannot start until she actively confirms. No hub manager can override this.
✓
CASE A: Receiver confirmed present → professional initiates timer. Service begins. No further action required.
⚠
CASE B: Different person present → timer lock activates. Booking cannot proceed to active service. Hub manager is notified automatically and contacts the customer to verify receiver identity. Professional receives an in-app prompt: "You are not required to enter. You will be paid in full if you choose to leave." The professional decides to enter or leave. The hub manager cannot coerce her to enter. Her decision is final.
✗
CASE C: No one present → timer lock activates. Professional does not enter. Hub manager notified. Full pay protection applies automatically. Booking is flagged for risk scoring.
Policy design note — pre-acceptance
When household context indicates elevated situational risk, specifically "Multiple members — all male", the briefing surfaces this prominently before the professional confirms travel. If she flags for reassignment, the booking returns to the hub manager. If no professional accepts, the hub manager communicates limited availability to the customer without disclosing the reason. Pronto does not refuse the customer. The professional exercises her right to decline without penalty or explanation.
Policy design note — pay protection
A professional who declines entry at the door, whether due to a receiver mismatch, household context concern, or any other safety assessment, is paid in full for the booking with no deduction, no rating impact, and no explanation required. This is not discretionary. It is a product-enforced policy. The economic lever that makes hub manager pressure effective must be removed by design, not by training.
4
Post-Service Professional Self-Report
Pronto Professional app · Post-service · Core Foundation for Customer Assessment
After the timer stops, professional completes a structured log in under 60 seconds within 15 minutes of the completed booking. Framed as a routine service record, not a complaint mechanism.
Field 1
Address confirmation
Pre-filled from booking. Professional confirms or corrects. Validates address accuracy across the booking base.
Field 2
Household composition observed
Who was actually home during the appointment. This is the ground-truth record.
Field 3
Household size estimate
1 / 2 / 3–4 / 5+ people. Builds demographic data on Pronto's actual customer household base over time.
Fields 4–5 (Internal only)
Environment note + Comfort rating
Additional notes on customer home environement and comfort rating on a scale of 1 to 5, never shown to customer.
Why this data matters
Cross-referencing the customer's household context declaration against the professional's post-service observation creates a ground-truth accuracy signal.

Consistent mismatches between declaration and observation, across multiple bookings at the same account, become the most reliable risk signal in the entire architecture, because it is based on actual observed reality, not probabilistic inference.
What these four interventions make possible
Every booking completed under this architecture generates two things Pronto does not currently have: a declared household record from the customer before the service, and a ground-truth observation from the professional after it.

Over time, that data creates a customer behaviour history that informs how subsequent bookings from the same account may be scored and handled.

Design principle
The professional is never shown a risk score or a negative label about a customer. What she is shown in her pre-arrival briefing is facts: the household context declaration, the booking type, the receiver identity, and whether this is a new or returning account.

A "Trusted account" badge appears only where a positive history has been established; its absence is not a warning, it is simply the absence of history. The professional reads the situation from what the platform knows and exercises her own judgement. Pronto never says "dangerous." It ensures she is never uninformed.

The regulatory context
India’s IT Ministry has taken cognisance of this issue (May 26, 2026). The DPDP Act 2023 requires consent to be specific and purpose-bound. “Consent for service recording is legally different from consent for AI training data use” — Kamesh Shekar, The Dialogue.

Proactively answering the open questions above can make Pronto the category definer rather than the category defendant.
4 structural interventions build the trust-first data architecture Pronto does not currently have.
Each intervention is based on consent design and data governance decisions, the minimum required to make any recording-adjacent feature defensible under DPDP Act 2023.
1
Purpose-Separated Consent Architecture
Consent design · Pre-booking · DPDP Act compliance requirement
Service recording consent and data commercialisation consent are legally distinct purposes under DPDP Act 2023. They must be collected separately, with specific purpose language for each. Under DPDP Act 2023, a single opt-in cannot legally cover both distinct purposes.

The first purpose → service transparency, where the customer receives footage for their own review.

The second → any downstream use of that footage or derived data for AI training (a legally distinct purpose under DPDP Act 2023).
Purpose A: Service transparency
Recording of your service for your personal review. Deleted within 48 hours. Only you can access. No third-party access of any kind.
Purpose B: AI / Research contribution
Anonymised data from your session may contribute to physical AI research. Separate, explicit consent required. Completely independent opt-in from Purpose A.
What this does
1. Makes the two consent purposes legally distinct and separately governed.
2. Eliminates the core DPDP purpose limitation risk.
3. Gives the customer genuine informed choice on each use case independently.
What this does not do
It does not prevent Pronto from pursuing a data business.

It simply requires that the data business be built on proper consent architecture rather than inherited from service-level consent.
Why purpose separation matters in practice
Industry research has shown that when recording consent is bundled with a financial incentive, the rate of opt-in rises significantly, raising questions about whether customers are making a genuinely independent choice about their data.

Under DPDP Act 2023, consent that is conditional on a discount or service benefit may not meet the threshold of freely given, specific, and informed consent. A consent architecture that separates purpose clearly, before any pricing incentive is introduced, is the structure most likely to hold under regulatory scrutiny.
2
Transparent Data Lifecycle Commitment
Customer app · Pre-booking and post-service · Trust signal
The customer should be able to see, in plain language, exactly what happens to anything captured during their service, at the moment of opt-in, not buried in terms and conditions. This is a display commitment, not a new data system.
During
Outward-facing video only. Faces auto-blurred in real time. A trust-first design would ensure the professional's device does not store footage locally.
2 hrs
Customer receives footage via in-app link. Accessible only to them. Platform access, if any, should be limited to dispute resolution and disclosed explicitly to the customer.
48 hrs
Footage deleted from all Pronto servers. Customer receives a deletion confirmation notification. Deletion should be verifiable, not just a policy claim.
Flag
⚠️ Escalation hold ⚠️: Cases where a pro has raised a safety concern, or a customer has reported theft or a conduct issue following a service, footage relevant to that booking should be held beyond the standard 48-hour window pending resolution.

  • Be triggered only by a formal complaint or safety flag raised by either party
  • Extend retention for the duration of the review, not indefinitely
  • Be disclosed to the customer at the point the hold is applied, not after resolution
  • Remain subject to the same purpose limitation as the rest of the architecture (footage held for dispute resolution cannot be repurposed for AI training or any other use)
  • Result in deletion as soon as the escalation is closed, with a deletion receipt provided to both parties.
Never
Third-party sharing without separate, explicit AI/ research contribution consent. No sharing with AI labs, research partners, or any entity outside the service transparency purpose.
3
Internal Governance: No analysis without explicit consent
Data governance · Policy design · New category standard
Footage can be deleted, what is concluded from it during that window cannot. Annotation, categorisation, quality assessment etc. all produce outputs that survive the source recording.

A trust-first architecture draws a hard line between processing that stays inside the service relationship and processing that that outlives the footage.
Recommendation under Purpose A (service transparency)
  • –Processing limited to quality verification, dispute resolution, professional feedback.
  • –No outputs retained beyond the 48-hour footage window.
  • –No model training, no customer or professional profiling.
  • –The footage expires and what was derived from it expires with it.
Recommendation under Purpose B (AI contribution — separate consent)
  • –Processing feeds a model/ a dataset/ a research output (that outlives the footage).
  • –What is processed, what is inferred, how long inferences are retained, all must be disclosed.
  • –Customer retains the right to request deletion from any training dataset.
  • –Legally required under DPDP Act 2023. The only basis for genuinely consensual AI contribution.

Not a legal compliance page, but a clear, human-readable record of what exists, what has been deleted, and what was shared.
View 1
Active sessions

View 2

The gap between the as-is and the proposed is a gap in public visibility. Each of the four interventions is a transparency commitment, not a product rebuild.

The platform that makes these commitments proactively, before they are required by regulation, earns the trust standard for the category.
How Pronto responds when a customer raises a data concern.
One principle: every concern is met with a verifiable record, not a policy claim.
Concern Response Outcome
Tier 1
Customer query about their data
What was captured, when deleted, who can access.
Resolved from "My Data" dashboard for customers, no human escalation required.
  • –Customer directed to their consent history and deletion log.
  • –If deletion log shows confirmed deletion: query closed.
  • –If within 48-hour window: footage still accessible → customer can view or delete immediately.
Query resolved within the platform.
  • –No support ticket required for standard data queries.
  • –Verifiable record means the customer can confirm for themselves without relying on a company claim.
Tier 2
Customer alleges consent violation
Claims data used for a purpose not consented to.
Human review initiated within 24 hours.
  • –Data privacy lead reviews consent record for the specific booking.
  • –Confirms which purposes were consented to and what data actions occurred.
  • –If violation found: immediate corrective action and disclosure to customer.
Customer receives written response within 72 hours.
  • –Response includes consent record, data action log, and outcome of review.
  • –If violation confirmed: customer offered remedy.
Tier 3
Regulatory inquiry or confirmed data misuse
Immediate regulatory cooperation protocol.
  • –Legal counsel engaged within 2 hours of formal notice.
  • –Full data audit initiated. All affected customers notified proactively.
  • –Pilot paused pending audit completion.
Transparent public disclosure.
  • –Company issues factual public statement within 48 hours of audit conclusion.
  • –Disclosure distinguishes between what was designed, what was operated, and what is being corrected.
