A Safety Architecture for Pronto at Scale

The professional is verified. The home she walks into is not.

This artefact documents the customer-side safety gap and recommends a set of interventions that builds accountability and creates the data foundation for a safer platform at scale.
All inputs from verified public sources · May 2026
The starting point
The professional earns her trust. The customer assumes theirs.
Pronto's supply side is the most rigorously verified in the category. Its demand side has no equivalent.

A customer can create an account with a mobile number, declare any name and any address, and request a verified woman professional enter a private residence, with the platform having no meaningful visibility into the environment she is entering.
The most important evidence
Women professionals have already developed informal safety heuristics to compensate. Some call customers before a booking to check whether a woman will be present at the residence. If no woman is present, they may decline.

This is a pronto professional-created workaround for what must ideally be a platform-level check.

Where we are starting from
The architecture recommended is a risk reduction and traceability system, not a prevention system.

While the larger goal is to enable prevention-grade customer safety architecture, the immediate aim is to solve for strong visibility and verification of the customer side.
Implementable now · Zero ops overhead · No third-party dependencies
4 low-tech interventions build the accountability infrastructure Pronto does not currently have.
Each intervention is self-contained, requires no new infrastructure, and generates no manual ops overhead.
1
GPS Hard Registration Gate
Registration · One-time · Zero ops overhead
Make GPS location confirmation non-bypassable at account creation.

If the user denies location access, registration does not complete. The user must pin their location on a map before the account is created. The GPS-registered location becomes the first data point in the traceability footprint.

Every registered account on Pronto has a real-world location on record. Anonymous bookings become traceable ones.
Applies to
Registration only. Repeat bookings at different addresses are legitimate and are not re-challenged.
Ops overhead
Nil. Product workflow change only.
GPS granted
User pins location → registration completes → GPS-anchored record created
GPS denied
Registration blocked until location access is enabled
2
Safety Commitment Moment + Household Context Declaration
First booking initiation + checkout · Net new to category
Displayed exactly when the customer intends to make the first booking
"Help us make this a safe and positive experience for everyone."
Pronto professionals are verified, trained, and trusted. We ask our customers to meet them with the same respect.
This is not a T&Cs wall. It is a single full-screen moment, warm, human, brief, that signals to the customer that the professional's safety is a value Pronto holds actively.

It creates psychological accountability at the exact moment the professional's safety becomes real.

It frames what immediately follows, the two household context questions, as care rather than interrogation/ friction.
Question 1 — Booking intent · Single select · Mandatory
"Who are you booking for?"
🏠 Myself — I will be present 📍 Someone else — I am booking remotely
If "Someone else" is selected: receiver name, receiver contact number, and full service address are captured as a linked accountability record. Currently these may exist as three disconnected fields in the app with no linkage to the booker's verified identity.
Question 2 — Household context · Single select · Mandatory
"Who will be home during the appointment?"
👩 Only I will be present 👩 Female resident only 👨 Male resident only 🧓 Elderly female resident 🧓 Elderly male resident 👫 Mixed household — female and male members 👨‍👨 Multiple members — all male
The "Only I will be present" option is shown only when Q1 = Myself. "Multiple members — all male" surfaces the one household composition that is most safety-relevant to a lone woman professional, without the platform making a blocking decision on that basis.
What this does
Removes the burden from the professional to independently assess the household situation before entering. The platform now carries that information systematically.

For remote bookings, creates a named accountability record linking receiver identity, contact number, and service address as a single declared unit.
What this does not do
It does not verify the declaration. A bad actor can lie. It is limited as a declared intent record and a data instrument, not a prevention mechanism.

A false declaration does not prevent an incident, but it creates a documented accountability footprint that did not previously exist.
3
Professional Pre-Arrival Briefing + Door Confirmation
Pronto Professional app · At dispatch and at arrival · Net new to category
Before the professional begins travel, her app shows a structured briefing: the receiver name and contact, whether this is a self-booking or a remote booking, and the full household context declaration.

Critically, the briefing includes a soft pre-acceptance step. The professional sees the household context before the booking is confirmed to her. She can accept or flag for reassignment. No explanation required, no formal complaint process, no penalty. The platform does not make the safety call. The professional does.

At arrival, before the countdown timer starts, the professional confirms presence at the door. The timer is a product-enforced lock. It cannot start until she actively confirms. No hub manager can override this.
✓
CASE A: Receiver confirmed present → professional initiates timer. Service begins. No further action required.
⚠
CASE B: Different person present → timer lock activates. Booking cannot proceed to active service. Hub manager is notified automatically and contacts the customer to verify receiver identity. Professional receives an in-app prompt: "You are not required to enter. You will be paid in full if you choose to leave." The professional decides to enter or leave. The hub manager cannot coerce her to enter. Her decision is final.
✗
CASE C: No one present → timer lock activates. Professional does not enter. Hub manager notified. Full pay protection applies automatically. Booking is flagged for risk scoring.
Policy design note — pre-acceptance
When household context indicates elevated situational risk, specifically "Multiple members — all male", the briefing surfaces this prominently before the professional confirms travel. If she flags for reassignment, the booking returns to the hub manager. If no professional accepts, the hub manager communicates limited availability to the customer without disclosing the reason. Pronto does not refuse the customer. The professional exercises her right to decline without penalty or explanation.
Policy design note — pay protection
A professional who declines entry at the door, whether due to a receiver mismatch, household context concern, or any other safety assessment, is paid in full for the booking with no deduction, no rating impact, and no explanation required. This is not discretionary. It is a product-enforced policy. The economic lever that makes hub manager pressure effective must be removed by design, not by training.
4
Post-Service Professional Self-Report
Pronto Professional app · Post-service · Core Foundation for Customer Assessment
After the timer stops, professional completes a structured log in under 60 seconds within 15 minutes of the completed booking. Framed as a routine service record, not a complaint mechanism.
Field 1
Address confirmation
Pre-filled from booking. Professional confirms or corrects. Validates address accuracy across the booking base.
Field 2
Household composition observed
Who was actually home during the appointment. This is the ground-truth record.
Field 3
Household size estimate
1 / 2 / 3–4 / 5+ people. Builds demographic data on Pronto's actual customer household base over time.
Fields 4–5 (Internal only)
Environment note + Comfort rating
Additional notes on customer home environement and comfort rating on a scale of 1 to 5, never shown to customer.
Why this data matters
Cross-referencing the customer's household context declaration against the professional's post-service observation creates a ground-truth accuracy signal.

Consistent mismatches between declaration and observation, across multiple bookings at the same account, become the most reliable risk signal in the entire architecture, because it is based on actual observed reality, not probabilistic inference.
What these four interventions make possible
Every booking completed under this architecture generates two things Pronto does not currently have: a declared household record from the customer before the service, and a ground-truth observation from the professional after it.

Over time, that data creates a customer behaviour history that informs how subsequent bookings from the same account may be scored and handled.

The most significant gap
The receiver field at checkout accepts any name, any number, entirely unverified and decoupled from the booking identity. The professional walks through a door not knowing who is on the other side.

The legitimate use case the receiver field serves
Working professionals booking Pronto for elderly parents in different addresses or cities. Caregivers arranging services for dependents.

The recommended flow preserves this use case while creating a named accountability record for every remote booking.
Internal to Pronto only
No professional enters a home cold. The platform has already mapped what she needs to know.
Every new and completed booking adds to what Pronto knows about a customer.

That accumulated history, including household declarations made, observations recorded by professionals, comfort signals logged, determines how every subsequent booking from that account is handled.

There are three possible account states.
Trusted Account
GPS-registered location matches booking address
Household context declaration consistent with professional observations across multiple bookings
Door confirmation: receiver match rate high
Comfort ratings provided by professional consistently at threshold or above
Repeat customer with established booking history
Professional sees "Trusted account" flag in her pre-arrival briefing before travel begins.

Standard dispatch.
New Account
First or second booking, no customer history yet
Household declaration completed but no/ limited observation data to cross-reference
No prior door confirmation outcomes on record
Professional sees no flag. Absence of a badge is not a warning, it is the absence of history.

Standard dispatch.
Account Under Review
Accumulated threshold of low comfort ratings from multiple professionals
Repeated declaration vs. observation mismatches confirmed by post-service reports
Door confirmation: repeated receiver mismatches or no-shows on record
Account placed on hold. No new bookings confirmed until hub manager review is complete.

Customer receives a neutral message, no score, no accusation, no explanation beyond that the team will be in touch.
Design principle
The professional is never shown a risk score or a negative label about a customer. What she is shown in her pre-arrival briefing is facts: the household context declaration, the booking type, the receiver identity, and whether this is a new or returning account.

A "Trusted account" badge appears only where a positive history has been established; its absence is not a warning, it is simply the absence of history. The professional reads the situation from what the platform knows and exercises her own judgement. Pronto never says "dangerous." It ensures she is never uninformed.

Post-threshold protocol: what happens when accumulated signals breach the defined limit
Triggered by accumulation of Tier 1 flags, not by a single incident. Three sequential steps.
▼
Step 1
Defined threshold of accumulated Tier 1 signals breached.
Automatic account hold
  • No new bookings can be confirmed.
  • Customer receives a neutral message: "We are currently unable to confirm new bookings for your account. Our team will be in touch shortly."
  • No accusation. No score disclosed. No explanation.
Hub manager review initiated
  • Hub manager receives full account history: declaration accuracy, comfort ratings, door confirmation outcomes, booking pattern, before making any decision.
Step 2
Hub manager review.
Hub manager phone outreach to customer
  • A brief direct conversation, not an interrogation.
  • Purpose is to give the customer an opportunity to provide context.
  • An elderly resident who cannot communicate clearly, a genuinely chaotic but safe household, a professional who may have had a personal issue, these surface in a conversation.
  • Hub manager exercises judgement.
Three possible outcomes
See Step 3 below.
Step 3
Hub manager decision following review.
Outcome A: reinstated with internal flag
  • No genuine safety concern found. Account restored.
  • Carries an internal flag: future bookings from this account are routed to experienced professionals only, with the full account history, prior comfort ratings, declaration accuracy, review outcome, in the pre-arrival briefing.
Outcome B: Permanent suspension & Outcome C: Police escalation
Outcome B:
  • Consistent and credible pattern confirmed. Account suspended.
  • Customer told account has been closed. No reason given beyond that Pronto reserves the right to suspend access.
  • No legal admission, no accusation.


Outcome C:
  • Any single booking that witnessed a formal safety incident bypasses this protocol entirely. Goes directly to Tier 2 / Tier 3 of the misconduct response detailed above.
Incident response
In-booking SOS protocol: what happens minute by minute
▼
T+0
Professional triggers SOS → Hub manager receives immediate alert with professional's last confirmed location (door confirmation GPS), customer account details, household context declaration, and receiver identity on record.
T+2 min
Hub manager calls professional → If answered: assesses situation, instructs exit or shelter. If unanswered: escalate immediately.
T+5 min
Hub manager begins physical movement toward address (1.5–2km polygon). Police call initiated simultaneously.
T+10 min
Customer account suspended → Traceability footprint: phone, GPS registration record, household declaration, payment method, flagged for police review. Declaration vs. observation history retrieved.
T+15 to 20 min
Hub manager arrives → Police briefed with full customer footprint. Formalising this SLA and closing the 15–20 minute physical response gap is the single most important operational complement to this architecture.
The safety proposition for professionals
1. Every customer has a verified location on record, a declared household context before they arrive, and a ground-truth log created after they complete a booking.
2. Patterns of concern trigger a human review, not an algorithm.
3. Confirmed misconduct by customers results in them being permanently banned.

This is the infrastructure that makes Pronto the platform professionals choose, and stay on.
