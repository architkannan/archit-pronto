Micromarket Ops Scorecard

5 simulated micromarkets, 5 key indicators. This scorecard defines the governance design: which indicators matter, what thresholds trigger action, and what the ops team does next.
All inputs from verified public sources
Weekly Review Instrument · Not a Real-Time Dashboard
Ownership: This scorecard is owned by the City Ops function. It covers the professional supply lifecycle, including deployment, productivity, and retention. Demand-side metrics (customer retention, repeat booking rate, slot conversion) are tracked in a companion instrument owned by Growth.

In production: each indicator is pre-populated every Monday from an overnight batch pull of booking data. Each city ops lead opens a most updated view of their micromarket before the weekly review begins, with Red markets already flagged.

To stress-test the framework: use the Simulate button on any indicator to test how the escalation logic responds under different readings before any data infrastructure is built.
Indicator definitions - read before reviewing micromarket data
Professional Coverage %
# of active professionals ÷ # of target professionals x 100
Ratio of active professionals to the target required for this micromarket at the 7-booking productivity benchmark. Shortfall = target - active, shown as an absolute number.
Why it matters:
A micromarket cannot fulfil its demand allocation if coverage is in Red. This is the deployment decision indicator, it tells the ops team where to send the next trained cohort before any other deployment decision is made.
Fulfilled Booking %
Completed bookings ÷ Accepted bookings x 100
Percentage of accepted bookings completed as promised (professional arrived, on time, and completed the service). A booking is fulfilled only when the professional logs check-out with no cancellation or no-show recorded.
Why it matters:
This is the customer promise. The Play Store review samples identifies unfulfilled bookings as the primary driver of 1-star ratings and churn. Below 92%, trust degrades faster than marketing can rebuild it.
Slot Availability %
Available slots ÷ Total possible slots x 100
Percentage of shift-hour slots open for booking at the point a customer requests. Measured daily, averaged weekly. A slot is available if a professional is scheduled, their shift covers that window, and no conflicting booking exists.
Why it matters:
If customers cannot find a slot they either wait or leave. Slot availability catches the supply constraint before the customer files a complaint. It is an earlier warning than the fulfilled booking rate.
Utilisation %
Actual bookings per professional per day ÷ 7 x 100
Actual bookings per professional per active shift day as a percentage of the 7-booking benchmark. Measured as the trailing 7-day average across all active professionals in the micromarket.
Why it matters:
Professional wages accrue across the full shift regardless of bookings completed. Every point below the benchmark is idle shift cost. At 4 bookings/day (the current platform average), Pronto is running at 57% of its own productivity standard of 7 bookings/day.
30-Day Professional Retention %
# of professionals active at day 30 ÷ # of professionals activated x 100
Percentage of professionals activated this month who are still active 30 days later. Active at day 30 = completed at least one booking in the prior 7 days. Platform-wide retention is 70%, meaning 30% of the active base churns monthly.
Why it matters:
Retention of professionals is the leading indicator of quality compounding. A micromarket with low retention is permanently resetting its experience base. When every churned professional is replaced by a less experienced one, the quality standard erodes with every booking.
Step 1
Read the system summary first. The four cards at the top give you the week's supply health headline: how many markets need immediate action, how many need monitoring, total professional shortfall across all markets, and the average fulfilled booking rate. If any market is in Red, the alert strip below the summary will tell you which ones.
Step 2
Select a micromarket card to open the detail panel. Each card shows all five indicators with traffic-light status. Click any card to see the full indicator breakdown, threshold bar, and the next best action. Every next best action names who acts, by when, and what they do, in a production environment. This becomes the basis for the weekly ops review agenda.
Step 3
Adjust sliders to test threshold calibration. Every indicator value is editable for demo purposes. Drag a slider to simulate a different reading and watch the status, escalation trigger, and system summary update in real time.
Step 4
Open indicator definitions for formula and context. The "Indicator definitions" button above opens a glossary panel with the full formula, definition, and business rationale for each indicator. Read this before calibrating thresholds or interpreting edge cases.
Verified
7 bookings/professional/day is the active micromarket productivity benchmark sourced from Moneycontrol, March 2026. It is used as the denominator in Utilisation % and as the basis for calculating target professionals in Professional Coverage.
Verified
70% monthly retention is the platform-wide figure from the CEO podcast, April 2026. The 30-Day Retention indicator uses 75% as its Green threshold, set slightly above the platform average to distinguish strong micromarkets from the baseline.
Verified
500+ bookings/day in the best micromarket (Moneycontrol, March 2026) is the basis for the Sector 45 Gurugram default values, which are calibrated to represent a best-in-class micromarket.
Assumed
All default indicator values are simulated. Micromarket names, indicator readings, target professional counts, and active professional counts are illustrative. They are set to span the realistic range of market maturity (best-in-class to struggling) so the framework can be stress-tested across scenarios. None of these values represent Pronto's actual operational state.
Assumed
Thresholds are hypotheses, not recommendations. The Green/Amber/Red cutoffs for each indicator (e.g. 92% for Fulfilled Booking Rate, 85% for Slot Availability Rate) are derived from the review corpus and public benchmark data. They have no Pronto-specific calibration. The correct thresholds should be set by Pronto using internal cohort data from established micromarkets.
Assumed
Weekly batch aggregation is the assumed data architecture. Each indicator is specified as a weekly aggregate, not a real-time feed. This reflects the realistic data infrastructure available at Pronto's current engineering maturity. A real-time dashboard would require a stream processing layer that does not yet exist.
Scope
Five micromarkets is a prototype sample, not a representative view. A production deployment would cover all active micromarkets across all cities. The five shown here are illustrative of the range of market maturity states. The framework scales to any number of micromarkets: the governance logic does not change.
Scope
Micromarket boundaries are assumed, not verified. The geographic polygon that defines a micromarket, which bookings belong to it, which professionals are assigned to it, is an internal operational decision. This prototype assumes boundaries exist and are stable. In practice, boundary changes would affect all five indicators simultaneously.
Scope
The Professional Coverage calculation assumes the target is known. Target professionals per micromarket = micromarket's target bookings per day divided by 7. If the micromarket-level booking target has not been formally allocated, the coverage indicator cannot be calculated. Establishing that allocation is a prerequisite for this indicator to go live.
Data
The escalation protocols are governance design, not operational standing orders. The "next best action" text for each indicator status is a specification of what the protocol should say. Adapting it to Pronto's actual ops team structure, role definitions, and escalation hierarchy requires an internal review before it becomes a live operating procedure.
Data
Circular coverage calculation risk. Professional Coverage uses target professionals as the denominator, which is itself derived from the bookings target. If the target is revised, the coverage percentage changes even if the number of active professionals stays the same. Reviewers should always read the absolute shortfall number alongside the percentage.
